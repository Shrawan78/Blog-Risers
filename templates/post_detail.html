The Slow Read: Why We're Forgetting How to Finish a Book

By Maya Okonkwo · September 24, 2026 · 6 min read

S

Somewhere in the last decade, "I'm reading a book" quietly became a claim rather than a fact. Ask around and most people will name something they started. Fewer will tell you what happens on the last page.

This isn't really about laziness. It's about the shape of our days. A notification arrives mid-paragraph, and the paragraph loses. Multiply that by every hour of every day and finishing anything long becomes an act of resistance rather than a default.

What actually changed

The book itself hasn't gotten harder. What's changed is the number of things competing for the same forty minutes on the couch. A feed is built to reward you every few seconds. A novel asks you to wait two hundred pages for the reward, and most of us have quietly renegotiated that deal without noticing.

None of this means the long-form habit is gone for good. It means it has to be chosen on purpose now, the way people once had to choose to exercise once their jobs stopped requiring physical labor. The book didn't change. The rest of the day did.

A smaller, more honest goal

The people I know who still finish books tend to share one habit: they stopped trying to read everywhere. One chair, one time of day, phone in another room. It's a small rule, but it's the difference between a book that lives on the nightstand for a year and one that gets read.
